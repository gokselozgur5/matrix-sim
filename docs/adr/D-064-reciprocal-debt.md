# D-064 — Reciprocal debt: resistance belongs to the mind; breach belongs to the actor

*In the context of making D-062's Human a causal subject, facing D-022's historical compression of resistance and systemic anomaly pressure into one film-facing ledger, we lean toward two non-interchangeable ledgers — epistemic resistance owned by the persistent mind and contract debt owed by the accountable actor or institution — and against resistance-only, system-only, or renamed legacy counters, to preserve agency, responsibility, replay and repair without assigning an imposed frame to its victim, accepting two conservation laws and a larger causal audit surface.*

## Context and Problem Statement

D-022 made a true and useful film mechanism: per-link residue feeds a global anomaly ledger until the system owes the story The One. D-062 asks a different question. Once a resident's situated interpretation is a necessary cause, a mismatch inside that resident and an obligation created by someone who constrained or deceived them cannot share one owner, one meaning or one discharge rule.

A deceived person may believe the deception and therefore carry little epistemic resistance while the deceiver still owes repair. A skeptical person may resist an honestly delivered fact while nobody has breached their contract. A model that collapses these cases turns disbelief into guilt, persuasion into payment, or system pressure into psychology.

The decision is therefore about ownership and conservation before storage: who carries each state, what event can change it, what survives reset, and what later causality may read it?

## Decision Drivers

* A harmed or deceived Human must never become responsible for an imposed frame merely because their mind reacted to it
* D-062's persistent subject needs epistemic continuity across avatars, links, pills, disconnect and reinsertion
* Agency violations need an accountable actor, named subject, named clause and explicit remedy
* Consent, refusal, uncertainty, deception, coercion, breach and repair must remain causally distinguishable
* Reset, persuasion, death and time must not impersonate institutional repair
* Existing film mechanics must retain their historical meaning rather than obtain a new ontology by rename
* Both state families must replay deterministically and stay on the correct side of D-013's no-upload boundary

## Considered Options

* Resistance only: one per-mind mismatch state stands in for both interpretation and obligation
* System debt only: only contract breach persists; epistemic tension is reconstructed or omitted
* Reciprocal two-ledger model: mind-owned resistance and actor/institution-owned contract debt have different events and conservation laws
* Explicit alternative: a different decomposition that still keeps coercion, disbelief, consent, contradiction and repair distinguishable

## Decision Outcome

Proposed option: "reciprocal two-ledger model", because epistemic state answers what the resident can believe or choose next while contract debt answers who owes what repair to whom. Neither question can truthfully substitute for the other. Final call in thread #1670 (phase gate).

The proposal keeps the units abstract on purpose. Deterministic integer quanta make replay and conservation testable, but later gated realization chooses psychology scales, severity tables, storage records and formulas.

### Consequences

* Good, because belief revision can change a mind without laundering a breach
* Good, because contract repair can settle an obligation without editing the subject's beliefs
* Good, because the same causal history can reconstruct both ledgers independently
* Good, because D-022's film machinery remains an honest historical mechanism
* Bad, because every breach and remedy needs more provenance than a global counter
* Bad, because two ledgers require an explicit seam between mind transition and institutional settlement

## Pros and Cons of the Options

### Resistance only

* Good, because the resident's interpretation remains local and causally useful
* Neutral, because a world can still observe aggregate resistance without treating it as obligation
* Bad, because coercion or deception has no persistent accountable owner when the victim does not resist
* Bad, because belief revision can accidentally look like payment

### System debt only

* Good, because breach, obligation and remedy can be audited institutionally
* Neutral, because ordinary uncertainty may remain transient when it changes no later choice
* Bad, because accumulated interpretation has no persistent state through which perception changes reachable intent
* Bad, because D-062's subject loop becomes dependent on reconstructing a mind from world-facing events

### Reciprocal two-ledger model

* Good, because ownership follows the question each state answers
* Good, because a single event may affect both ledgers without making them equal or mutually dischargeable
* Good, because responsibility remains with the accountable actor while epistemic history remains with the Human
* Bad, because every cross-ledger relationship must cite one underlying causal event rather than share a mutable number

### Explicit alternative

* Good, because a better decomposition remains admissible if it preserves all required distinctions
* Neutral, because more than two ledgers may eventually be useful for trust, sanctions or policy
* Bad, because an alternative without owner and conservation laws merely hides the original collapse behind more names
