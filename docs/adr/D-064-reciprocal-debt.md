# D-064 — Reciprocal debt: resistance belongs to the mind; breach belongs to the actor

*In the context of making D-062's Human a causal subject, facing D-022's historical compression of resistance and systemic anomaly pressure into one film-facing ledger, we lean toward two non-interchangeable ledgers — epistemic resistance owned by the persistent mind and contract debt owed by the accountable actor or institution — and against resistance-only, system-only, or renamed legacy counters, to preserve agency, responsibility, replay and repair without assigning an imposed frame to its victim, accepting two conservation laws and a larger causal audit surface.*

## Context and Problem Statement

D-022 made a true and useful film mechanism: per-link residue feeds a global anomaly ledger until the system owes the story The One. D-062 asks a different question. Once a resident's situated interpretation is a necessary cause, a mismatch inside that resident and an obligation created by someone who constrained or deceived them cannot share one owner, one meaning or one discharge rule.

A deceived person may believe the deception and therefore carry little epistemic resistance while the deceiver still owes repair. A skeptical person may resist an honestly delivered fact while nobody has breached their contract. A model that collapses these cases turns disbelief into guilt, persuasion into payment, or system pressure into psychology.

The decision is therefore about ownership and conservation before storage: who carries each state, what event can change it, what survives reset, and what later causality may read it?

## Decision Drivers

* A harmed or deceived Human must never become responsible for an imposed frame merely because their mind reacted to it
* D-062's persistent subject needs epistemic continuity across avatars, links, pills, disconnect and reinsertion
* Agency violations need an accountable actor, named subject, named clause and explicit remedy
* Consent, refusal, uncertainty, deception, coercion, breach and repair must remain causally distinguishable
* Reset, persuasion, death and time must not impersonate institutional repair
* Existing film mechanics must retain their historical meaning rather than obtain a new ontology by rename
* Both state families must replay deterministically and stay on the correct side of D-013's no-upload boundary

## Considered Options

* Resistance only: one per-mind mismatch state stands in for both interpretation and obligation
* System debt only: only contract breach persists; epistemic tension is reconstructed or omitted
* Reciprocal two-ledger model: mind-owned resistance and actor/institution-owned contract debt have different events and conservation laws
* Explicit alternative: a different decomposition that still keeps coercion, disbelief, consent, contradiction and repair distinguishable

## Decision Outcome

Proposed option: "reciprocal two-ledger model", because epistemic state answers what the resident can believe or choose next while contract debt answers who owes what repair to whom. Neither question can truthfully substitute for the other. Final call in thread #1670 (phase gate).

The proposal keeps the units abstract on purpose. Deterministic integer quanta make replay and conservation testable, but later gated realization chooses psychology scales, severity tables, storage records and formulas.

### Consequences

* Good, because belief revision can change a mind without laundering a breach
* Good, because contract repair can settle an obligation without editing the subject's beliefs
* Good, because the same causal history can reconstruct both ledgers independently
* Good, because D-022's film machinery remains an honest historical mechanism
* Bad, because every breach and remedy needs more provenance than a global counter
* Bad, because two ledgers require an explicit seam between mind transition and institutional settlement

### Confirmation

The decision record must name, for each chosen ledger, its owner, cause, unit, accrual, discharge, persistence boundary and observable consequence. It must classify D-022's `AcceptanceLoop.personalResidue` and `AnomalyLedger` without renaming them, add reciprocal links to D-022, and name the later realization responsibilities for resistance, breach and repair.

The decision PR is documentation-only. DocLint must prove record/index/ROADMAP agreement; DocFigures and DocsRoster must remain green; daemon selftests and the canonical digest leash must show that no world bytes changed. An adversarial review must try reset-as-repair, belief-as-payment, victim-blame, global-owner and legacy-rename counterexamples.

## Pros and Cons of the Options

### Resistance only

* Good, because the resident's interpretation remains local and causally useful
* Neutral, because a world can still observe aggregate resistance without treating it as obligation
* Bad, because coercion or deception has no persistent accountable owner when the victim does not resist
* Bad, because belief revision can accidentally look like payment

### System debt only

* Good, because breach, obligation and remedy can be audited institutionally
* Neutral, because ordinary uncertainty may remain transient when it changes no later choice
* Bad, because accumulated interpretation has no persistent state through which perception changes reachable intent
* Bad, because D-062's subject loop becomes dependent on reconstructing a mind from world-facing events

### Reciprocal two-ledger model

* Good, because ownership follows the question each state answers
* Good, because a single event may affect both ledgers without making them equal or mutually dischargeable
* Good, because responsibility remains with the accountable actor while epistemic history remains with the Human
* Bad, because every cross-ledger relationship must cite one underlying causal event rather than share a mutable number

### Explicit alternative

* Good, because a better decomposition remains admissible if it preserves all required distinctions
* Neutral, because more than two ledgers may eventually be useful for trust, sanctions or policy
* Bad, because an alternative without owner and conservation laws merely hides the original collapse behind more names

## Accepted Outcome — Two ledgers, never symmetrical blame

The Architect accepted the reciprocal two-ledger option in thread #1670 on 2026-08-19 as part of the verdict “üçü de kabul.” This section records that verdict without deleting or rewriting the proposal it answered.

The word *debt* does not make the two states interchangeable. Epistemic resistance describes a persistent Human's unresolved interpretation. Contract debt describes what an accountable actor or institution owes named subjects after agency was constrained, deceived or bypassed. One underlying event may contribute evidence to both, but neither state can pay, overwrite or morally explain the other.

### Mind-owned epistemic resistance

| Property | Accepted law |
|---|---|
| Owner | One persistent Human across avatars, links, pills, disconnect and reinsertion |
| Cause | A cited percept, memory or interpretation contradiction actually available to that Human |
| Unit | Deterministic integer evidence quanta; the later mind decision chooses scale and bounds |
| Accrual/revision | Only an ordered canonical receipt plus a mind interpretation or revision transition may change it |
| Discharge | Corroboration, belief revision, memory reconsolidation or repair of the experienced contradiction; no unconditional time decay |
| Persistence | Survives avatar, link and system reset while the Human persists; biography retains the historical transition after death or archive |
| Consequence | May change reachable goals and intents through the mind loop; never directly mutates a world or becomes institutional blame |

Resistance is not guilt, disobedience, loyalty or moral worth. Ordinary uncertainty may remain uncertainty. Refusal is a choice rather than a debt increment. Consent may coexist with doubt. A deceived Human may carry low resistance because the deception worked; that fact says nothing about what the deceiver owes.

### Actor-owned contract debt

| Property | Accepted law |
|---|---|
| Owner | The accountable actor, system or institution toward a named subject set and named Human Contract clause |
| Cause | A canonical breach entry naming initiator, responsible owner, subjects, consent class, act/effect provenance and severity |
| Unit | Deterministic integer breach points; later decisions own severity and duration tables |
| Accrual | Informed deception, coercion, unauthorized bypass or unremedied constraint; uncertainty alone and informed revocable consent do not accrue it |
| Discharge | Only a named repair, restoration, compensation, acknowledged release or adjudicated remedy applied to the same obligation |
| Persistence | No time decay; survives disconnect, reboot, persuasion, editing, killing or resetting the harmed mind |
| Consequence | Changes obligations, sanctions, access, institutional trust and future policy; never the subject's guilt or beliefs |

The conservation law is exact for each obligation identity `(responsible owner, subject set, clause, breach lineage)`:

```text
debt[obligation] after
  = debt[obligation] before
  + named BREACH entries for obligation
  - applied REPAIR entries for obligation

0 <= applied repair <= outstanding debt for that obligation
0 <= debt[obligation]
```

A repair cannot cancel another owner's or another subject's obligation, and overcompensation is a separate transfer or policy event rather than negative debt. A reset may move or restore storage but cannot emit `REPAIR`. Forgiveness may inform an acknowledged release; coercion cannot manufacture one. An explanation that the system had no alternative may affect adjudication but does not erase ownership of the imposed consequence.

### Causal distinctions

`UNCERTAIN`, `CONSENTED`, `REFUSED`, `DECEIVED`, `COERCED`, `IMPOSED`, `BREACHED` and `REPAIRED` are distinct event classifications. Consent is informed, specific and revocable; silence is not consent. Deception describes the delivery/knowledge relation, coercion the constrained choice set, breach the violated obligation and repair the later settlement. One label cannot stand in for another merely because the same scene contains both.

### Existing mechanics retain their names

* `AcceptanceLoop.personalResidue`, birth-key thresholds and spikes remain historical film mechanics and simulation-pressure telemetry. They are not the resident's new belief state.
* `AnomalyLedger` remains the system-instability book that drives the film specimen. It is not contract debt.
* The One's overflow cycle remains accepted historical behavior under the film profile.
* Later adapters may read these books as world facts, but neither becomes a new ledger through rename, field migration or shared storage.

### Realization responsibilities

The later implementation tree must keep four responsibilities separate:

1. **Mind transition** owns epistemic resistance and its receipt/revision history on the persistent Human side.
2. **Committed action/effect grammar** owns breach classification and names initiator, responsible owner, subject, clause and provenance.
3. **Institutional settlement** owns contract obligations and the only valid repair/discharge entries.
4. **Film compatibility** owns read-only adapters and regression evidence for `AcceptanceLoop` and `AnomalyLedger`; it may not reinterpret their stored values.

D-066 defines the causal seam between the first three. D-065 supplies the first inhabited witness in which mind history, an attributable act, social consequence and later perception can prove the seam end to end.

### Accepted falsifiers

* Editing a belief lowers contract debt → fail
* Killing or resetting the subject clears contract debt → fail
* Time alone clears contract debt → fail
* Refusal increments guilt or resistance by definition → fail
* A global debt counter cannot name owner, subject, clause and cause → fail
* A mind transition changes resistance without an ordered receipt/revision cause → fail
* Renaming `personalResidue` or `AnomalyLedger` satisfies either new ledger → fail
* Replaying one causal history cannot independently reconstruct both ledger deltas → fail

### Accepted Confirmation

This acceptance flips the record, index and Human Foundation gate together; adds the reciprocal D-022 disposition; preserves the pre-verdict proposal; and changes no Java, digest, snapshot, workflow or probe contract. DocLint, DocFigures, DocsRoster, daemon selftests, the digest leash and an adversarial decision review confirm the documentation unit.

Realization confirmation is stricter: an exhaustive event/handler census must account for every resistance, breach and repair delta; reset/death/persuasion counterexamples must leave contract debt conserved; and the same declared inputs must replay both ledgers byte-identically. No implementation issue may merge both ledgers into one owner or let either mutate the world outside D-066's committed causal path.
