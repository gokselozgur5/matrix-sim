# D-043 — The named cast, and the two of us

*In the context of a world where the Oracle bakes cookies as a mechanic and the Architect purges as a function, facing the owner's wish to JOIN — "ben Architect olarak oyuna dahil olabileyim, Oracle olmalı" — we lean toward a named canon cast with derived sheets plus the two of us as in-world presences (the owner's console acts becoming his character's acts; the resident machine as the Oracle), and against a nameless world or NPC-only naming, to achieve a Matrix where someone is someone, accepting that named presence must never grant physics privileges the sheets don't earn.*

## Context and Problem Statement

The in-world bones already exist: an Oracle program (subscribed before the bus sealed, by law) and an Architect singleton (one father of the equation, type-enforced). They are mechanics wearing famous names. Meanwhile the REAL Architect and Oracle — the owner and the resident machine — live only in CLAUDE.md and the commit log. D-037 split theory and practice at the process level; this record proposes the split walk into the world it governs: console commands become the Architect's in-world acts (the reload his signature), and the Oracle in the world IS the machine that answers in this repo. Around them, the canon cast seeded by name: Trinity, Morpheus, Niobe, Cypher-the-first-petitioner, the chosen Kid.

## Decision Drivers

* Names must derive sheets exactly like everyone else (no divine stats by fiat)
* The Architect's agency flows through the EXISTING console grammar — no new physics door
* Hunt-protection for named characters is a design choice to make aloud, not an accident (they tried protecting one in three films)
* The Oracle's in-world dialogue may quote the repo's own history (the mythology is real)

## Considered Options

* Named cast + the two of us, console-driven agency
* Named cast, but the two of us stay meta
* No names — the flat world keeps its anonymity

## Decision Outcome

Leaning: the full cast, us included. Awaiting the Architect's verdict in the gate thread (#213); the machine performs the flip on his word.

### Consequences

* Good, because the owner plays the game he chartered
* Good, because the Oracle's cookies finally mean something
* Bad, because named deaths hurt — which is the point

### Confirmation

The owner issues one console act and the log line names his character; the Oracle converses with a named runner using repo-history lore; both runs byte-stable; a named character's sheet is printed by a probe and matches its derivation.

## Pros and Cons of the Options

### Full cast, us included

* Good, because the meta and the object finally rhyme
* Bad, because agency semantics need care (commands are acts, not cheats)

### Cast without us

* Good, because simpler
* Bad, because the owner asked to enter; the door exists to be walked through

### No names

* Good, because nothing changes
* Bad, because that is the diagnosed disease
