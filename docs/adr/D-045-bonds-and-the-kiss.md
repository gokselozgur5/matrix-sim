# D-045 — Bonds, and the kiss

*In the context of a messiah born as a ledger debt and dead when accounting closes, facing the diagnosis that the ledger exists but love does not ("ledger var, aşk yok"), we lean toward bonds as real-side relations on character sheets plus the Room 303 clause — D-013's death law gains its ONE canonical exception: a bonded mind observing a death may pay it back — and against keeping death absolute or making love decorative, to achieve the trilogy's third force as bookkeeping, accepting that an exception to the bridge law must be narrower than every skeptic's imagination.*

## Context and Problem Statement

The mind-body rule (D-013) is the repo's oldest law: the avatar dies, the link flatlines the brain, no exceptions — four phases of skeptics enforced it to zero ghosts. The films' most iconic beat breaks exactly this law exactly once: Room 303, a death unwritten because someone loved him. In our grammar that is not magic; it is a TRANSACTION — the only force allowed to unwrite the ledger's most final entry. Bonds live real-side (two MINDS — the jack owns mind-body, bonds own mind-mind), on the sheets D-042 defines, and the payback must COST (a consumed bond, a broken will, a residue surge — the gate decides).

## Decision Drivers

* The exception must be singular, gated, and loud — one clause, not a mechanic family
* Love must cost: a free resurrection is a broken economy
* Bond formation semantics decide the world's warmth (seeded vs earned)
* Every skeptic probe that guards D-013 (LinkAudit, OneTrace) must learn the clause and still report zero GHOSTS

## Considered Options

* Bonds + the Room 303 clause (payback with cost)
* Bonds as lore only (no mechanical clause)
* No bonds

## Decision Outcome

Accepted by the owner's verdict, 2026-08-12, in session — *"hepsine agreed kanka barajı aç"* — all thirteen Season Three gates in one breath, the same word that opened Season Two; recorded in the gate thread.

*Recorded before the verdict, kept unedited:* Leaning: the clause, with a real price. Awaiting the Architect's verdict in the gate thread (#215); the machine performs the flip on his word.

### Consequences

* Good, because the trilogy's engine (love vs accounting) finally compiles
* Good, because the One's death at Machine City stays canon — HIS bond story is the negotiation, not the kiss
* Bad, because the bridge law's probes all need the clause taught to them

### Confirmation

A scripted scenario (probe or seed hunt): a bonded runner dies, the payback fires, the brain lives, the cost is booked, LinkAudit reports the resurrection as CLAUSE not GHOST, and the double run is byte-stable.

## Pros and Cons of the Options

### The clause with cost

* Good, because death matters MORE when one door out exists and it is priced
* Bad, because the narrowness must be defended forever

### Lore-only bonds

* Good, because zero risk to D-013
* Bad, because the diagnosis stands unanswered

### No bonds

* Good, because the law stays pristine
* Bad, because the simulation stays hermitic — mechanically perfect, emotionally empty
